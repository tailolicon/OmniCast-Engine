P1 + P2.0 of brief §15: see how a video is cut, run the channel, gate the output

Four packages, each answering one item the strategic review says is missing, and
each wired to something in production rather than left as a library.

§5 AUDIOVISUAL FORENSICS — analytics/av_forensics.py

The review calls this the largest gap: the system could read what a competitor
SAID and had never looked at how the video was CUT. Now measured from the file:
shot boundaries, shot durations, cuts per minute, motion per shot, colour mood,
silence, the music/SFX bed, loudness.

Dissolves get their own detector rather than a lower scene threshold, because
any threshold low enough to catch a crossfade invents boundaries inside every
moving shot. A linear blend has a signature nothing else has: the picture
changes completely, every step is an equal share of that change, and the middle
frame is the average of the two ends. Windows are swept from 0.4s to 3s and the
tolerances are fractions of the change, so fade length and contrast both cancel.

What it cannot do is listed with reasons — kinetic typography, callout
semantics, character consistency, camera framing, talking-head vs b-roll, joke
beats, thumbnail payoff, SFX taxonomy. All need a vision model. Text overlay is
a proxy, marked `inferred`, and never becomes the blueprint's
`text_overlay_freq`.

§11 CHANNEL STRATEGY — strategy/

Thesis, content architecture, stage gates, experiment portfolio, audience
journey, shorts->long funnel, and all twelve long-term metrics — including the
ones that cannot be computed, each naming what would unblock it. A dashboard
showing nine of twelve teaches its reader there are nine.

Declared, never generated. A thesis an LLM writes agrees with whatever the
channel already does, so it can never be evidence that the channel drifted. A
stage gate with no data returns `unknown`, not `fail`: killing an experiment
over a number nobody collected is how a system kills its own best bets.

§8/§9 QUALITY AND BENCHMARK GATES — quality/

Twelve gates, no fixed scores; a gate with no input is `unknown`, which is a
third state and the one that hides. The benchmark comparison refuses a headline
similarity figure until §8's own preconditions are met, and lists the material
that must NOT be copied — presenter likeness, licensed music, archival footage,
brand marks, voice identity — as unscored, because scoring them would reward
getting closer to something we cannot ship.

§7.1 ANIMATION FOUNDATION — animation/  (P2.0, NOT P2)

The review's sentence: generating AI images and crossfading them is not
animation. This is the subsystem's specification plus the parts that are pure
computation — character bible and continuity, easing, anticipation, volume-
preserving squash-and-stretch, comic timing, viseme timing.

IT RENDERS NOTHING, AND SAYS SO. `readiness()` reports `renders_frames: False`
and names rigging, prop interaction, camera choreography and drawing as absent.
This does not close P2; it is the foundation P2 needs.

The router now FAILS CLOSED on it: a `character_animation` string in a config
used to produce `mode=animation, was_substituted=False` for a pipeline with
nothing able to draw a frame, and the scene then fell through to a generated
image — the exact "AI images plus a crossfade" the review rejects, wearing the
label of the thing it is not. ANIMATION is routable only when a renderer is
registered and healthy, which today it is not.

SEVEN REVIEW ROUNDS, AND WHAT THEY CAUGHT

Every round found the same class of defect: a module that is correct in
isolation, wired so its answer changes nothing — or changes something for the
worse. That is worth stating plainly, because it is the pattern, not a list of
unrelated bugs.

* The quality gates ran AFTER the audit sidecar was written and never fed their
  verdict back into `passed`, so a video with a FAILED gate went to the approval
  queue. They now run inside `inspect_product`, before the sidecar.
* Then the FIX deadlocked it: `needs_human` also became an audit issue, so a
  finance video failed its own render — and it failed BEFORE the step that
  queues the video for approval, while nothing else writes `human_reviewed`.
  The flag blocked the only route to the review that clears it, and finance and
  health could not be produced at all. FAIL now blocks the render; `needs_human`
  lets the render succeed, flags the product, and blocks PUBLISH.
  `POST /api/products/{id}/review` records reviewer, timestamp and the SHA-256
  of the cut, so re-rendering invalidates the approval instead of inheriting
  it.
* `is_ymyl` was never set by anything, so it defaulted to False and every
  finance and health video passed the human gate reporting "not YMYL". It is now
  inferred from the channel's niche; when even that is unreadable the gate is
  `unknown`, never a pass.
* The dissolve detector was tuned to one fade length and one contrast — the
  arithmetic pinned it to 0.75-1.5s fades with a luma change of 25-60 of 255, so
  it missed almost every real dissolve, including this project's own 0.5s house
  default, while still publishing `shots: measured`.
* `benchmark.headline` was unreachable for every possible input, because
  `perceived_quality` — which is not machine-measurable by definition — was
  counted among the unmeasured. §8's threshold could never fire.
* `ebur128=framelog=quiet` is invalid before ffmpeg 6.1. The filter works; the
  option did not, and the emitted note blamed the build.
* `bool("no")` is True, which passed a compliance gate on `"false"` and
  published a benchmark headline on `blind_human_comparison="no"`.
* `squash_and_stretch` rounded both axes independently and broke the one
  invariant it exists for; at 1e6 one axis rounded to a literal 0.0.
* `OVERSHOOT_AMOUNT = 0.10` delivered 0.001, because the bump was multiplied by
  a term that is ~0 exactly where the bump peaks.
* A viseme track ran backwards on out-of-order or overlapping transcript cues.
* A Korean line produced one `rest` cue — a character who never opens their
  mouth — and this repo ships Korean voice configuration.
* `scoring_calibration` carried its own `_num` and diverged in exactly the two
  ways the shared one exists to prevent: `"1e400"` became `inf` and reached the
  report, and `10**400` raised `OverflowError` out of `summarize`.

Seven private `_num` implementations across seven modules were the source of
most of the above; there is now one, in `shared/numbers.py`, with `num`,
`count`, `rate`, `ratio` and `flag`.

Rounds three to six, all wiring:

* Competitor AV forensics had no production caller — it was measured on demand
  and never on the path that builds a blueprint.
* Production modes collapsed back into the two legacy visual types on the way to
  the renderer, so a scene classified INFOGRAPHIC OVERWROTE a good storyboard
  entry with a generated image, which this project's own image provider warns
  cannot render readable charts or text. Naming a scene correctly and then making
  the video worse is worse than not naming it.
* Title and thumbnail generation bypassed the scoped intel gate the writer had
  already been fixed to use.
* `depicts_real_events` was never passed, so the disclosure branch was dead code.
* `competitor_intel_required` still failed OPEN: `CompetitorIntelRequired` was
  caught alongside every other exception.
* `screen_capture` was granted for free, while the router's own
  `APPROXIMATED_MODES` says there is no screen-recording step in the pipeline.
* The forensics flag read only `os.environ`, so setting it in `.env` did nothing
  on CLI runs; and `socket_timeout` is not a wall clock, so a slow-drip download
  could run unbounded. Now `subprocess.run(timeout=)`.
* `IntelDecision` is a frozen dataclass and `decision.scope_level = level` raised
  inside a broad handler, so every caller saw a blank scope level.

Round six, the last three:

* The fail-closed error escaped `generate_clickbait` and was caught again by
  `render_real_video`'s `except Exception`, which logged a warning and printed
  DONE. The channel's declaration became a no-op one frame further out. There is
  now a named clause BEFORE the broad one: it prints BLOCKED, writes
  `packaging_blocked` and `publishable=False` to the product metadata, and
  re-raises. The MP4 exists; it has no approved packaging and must not ship.
* Fixing the ROUTER did not fix `stack_fit`, because the topic scorer does not
  call the router — it reads `PIPELINE_UNSUPPORTED_PRODUCTION`, where
  `screen_capture` was missing. A software tutorial still looked producible at
  the moment the system decides what to make. And the test that was supposed to
  cover this was named "does not reach the topic scorer" while only exercising
  the router: the same anti-pattern this commit message criticises elsewhere.
  There is now a test that builds a real ChannelProfile and runs TopicScorer.
* Packaging re-derived the content pillar by classifying the first 2000
  characters of the script, so a video the scorer had already filed under
  `annuities` could take the `social_security` thumbnail playbook. `pillar_id`
  now comes from `TopicBrief.pillar_id` through the product metadata; the
  classifier remains only as a labelled fallback for callers with no brief.

Round seven, the same defect on the WRITING side:

* `meta.json["pillar_id"]` had a reader and no writer. `_step_script` never
  wrote it, so in production the renderer's read always came back empty and
  packaging fell through to the classifier every single time — round six was
  correct on paper and inert in fact. All four scope dimensions are now written
  from the brief, not just the one that was being discussed.
* `publishable=False` was a flag, not a gate. The MP4 is registered as the
  channel's newest render BEFORE packaging fails, so the operator could still
  queue or upload the very artifact marked unpublishable.
  `products.publish_blockers()` is now checked in three places:
  `/api/publish/{id}` (409, and no approval row is created),
  `/api/upload/{id}` (the shorter path to YouTube), and
  `platforms.service.publish_approval_row()` — nearest the network call, so a
  row queued before the gate existed does not slip past it. `force` does NOT
  override it: `force` is for compliance false positives, and this flag is not a
  heuristic. It is deliberately NOT in `release_issues()`, because that gates
  the RENDER and a re-render is the repair path; a test pins that separation so
  it does not get tidied away into the deadlock this project already shipped
  once.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLOSE

P1.2 has no `series` or `packaging profile`, and `shorts_to_long_funnel` has no
caller because nothing declares a short's target video yet. P1.3's headline
similarity is permanently withheld because SFX placement and the music energy
curve have no analyser — honest, and it means P1.3 is an evaluation framework
rather than a finished benchmark gate. P2 needs a renderer. IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS
§4c lists all four.

3.12 is obtainable there, so 9 modules do not collect. tests/unit, run in three
batches: 1777 passing, 8 failing, 6 skipped, 9 collection errors; the other test
directories: 88 passing, 1 failing, 5 skipped. Every failure is a property of
the sandbox — `Path.walk` and backslashes in f-strings are 3.12+, the Claude CLI

Diffusion, and the Gemini family was already failing before this work. None of
them touch the code in this change. NOT verified on the target machine; the
numbers to trust are the ones measured there.

Every gate added in rounds six and seven was verified by REMOVING the fix and
watching the test go red; five of the ten round-seven tests fail without it, and
the other five cover the storage helper, which was not reverted.
